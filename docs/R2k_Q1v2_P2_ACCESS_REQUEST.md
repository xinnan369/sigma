**Subject** — LasHeR archive scope and capture metadata for independent academic research

Dear Dr. Sun and the LasHeR team,

I am [name] from [institution]. Our project studies thermal image quality using a frozen RGB–thermal validation protocol. We are assessing whether LasHeR can support a noncommercial, independent evaluation.

Could you confirm the canonical aligned archive version and checksums, and whether the MMIC Research-Only Use License covers that specific archive? Our planned use is private local storage, byte and visual duplicate auditing, local synthetic Gaussian blur derivation, and publication of aggregate statistics and protocol descriptions with attribution. We do not plan to redistribute image data. Please advise of any additional restrictions on archiving or deleting originals, derived images and private audit records, and any separate terms of the download provider.

Could you share a small metadata-only manifest with sequence IDs and release variants, visible/thermal pair IDs or counts, expected and missing-frame records, platform/sensor IDs, capture timestamps with timezone or collection-day/night annotations, and pseudonymous site/scene/route/session keys? If available, please include which clips are excerpts of a continuous recording and which share routes, backgrounds or source recordings with RGBT234/RGBT210 or other releases. Pseudonymous keys are sufficient for grouping.

Could you also confirm original and released image dimensions, channel counts, stored precision, thermal intensity processing (including any AGC), and available calibration or full-frame registration evidence for each platform? For FL-positive sequences, a frame-level record of missing, frozen, or blank captures would help us verify usable intact pairs. If you have frame-level or modality-specific MB annotations, please clarify their scope. We would keep any natural motion blur separate from our synthetic labels.

Thank you,
[name / affiliation / contact]
